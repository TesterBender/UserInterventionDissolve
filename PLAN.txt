# Collaborative Manuscript Protocol with Hidden Interaction Boundaries

Design document — V3, September 2026

## 1. Summary

This protocol enables continuous long-form fiction in which one designated character is authored externally by a human collaborator while the rest of the manuscript is generated by a language model.

The model is never told that the external character is special.

Instead, ownership is enforced structurally:

1. generation stops before the model can commit the external character's tag;
2. the collaborator supplies that character's block;
3. the supplied text is incorporated into the manuscript as ordinary manuscript content;
4. the next model-visible request reconstructs the mutable manuscript so prior live handoffs no longer appear as separate conversational turns;
5. manuscript spans are periodically frozen into arbitrary transport chunks whose boundaries are deliberately unrelated to the external character or major narrative structure.

The model therefore sees a manuscript repeatedly continued, not a conventional exchange between itself and the author of one particular character.

The protocol is host-agnostic. It may be implemented by a standalone client, extension, middleware layer, proxy, userscript, custom frontend, or any environment capable of reconstructing the model-visible request.

The central principle is:

> The model should experience a continuous causal world, not a sequence of opportunities to respond to a collaborator.

---

## 2. Goals

The protocol aims to:

* prevent the model from writing the externally owned character;
* enforce ownership mechanically rather than through obedience to a prompt;
* conceal the relationship between that character and real user/API turn structure;
* prevent collaboration frequency from becoming narrative pacing;
* reduce user-character gravity and binary roleplay volley;
* preserve manuscript continuity across arbitrary generation boundaries;
* allow long stretches in which the external character is absent or inactive;
* allow dense passages in which the collaborator intervenes frequently;
* preserve an append-only frozen prefix suitable for caching where supported;
* remain independent of any particular provider or frontend.

It does **not** attempt to:

* hide all stylistic evidence that one character may have a different author;
* guarantee that the model never constrains the external character indirectly through the environment;
* provide schema-validated output;
* eliminate every possible narrative-quality failure;
* require a specific API message format.

---

## 3. The actual concealment invariant

The protocol does not require perfect authorship non-identifiability.

A human-written character may naturally be more concise, surprising, inconsistent, resistant, or long-range intentional than model-written characters. A sufficiently capable observer might infer that the character has a distinct source.

That is acceptable.

The required property is narrower:

> **The compiled model-visible history must not preserve a recoverable mapping between the external character and real collaboration or generation boundaries.**

In particular, the model should not be able to infer from persistent history:

* which manuscript boundaries corresponded to human interventions;
* how many live generations produced a passage;
* which character appearance caused a generation stop;
* whether an external block came from a stop or a voluntary barge-in;
* how frequently the collaborator interacted with the host.

Many different live interaction histories should collapse to the same or functionally equivalent model-visible manuscript history.

This is **interaction-topology non-identifiability**.

---

## 4. Layers

The system has five relevant layers.

### Fiction

The story itself:

* characters;
* dialogue;
* setting;
* causality;
* chronology;
* atmosphere;
* perspective.

Visible to the model.

### Agency grammar

The rules governing how fictional agents commit actions, speech, decisions, and interior states.

Visible to the model.

### Real authorship

Who physically supplied each piece of text:

* model;
* collaborator;
* editor;
* regeneration process.

Hidden as implementation metadata.

### Live transport

Temporary machinery:

* model requests;
* hard stops;
* continuation-control turns;
* stream boundaries;
* captured user input.

This may exist transiently but should be normalized aggressively.

### Canonical history

The reconstructed manuscript history actually shown to the model across later requests.

This is the persistent conditioning surface.

It should preserve fiction and agency while discarding collaboration topology.

---

## 5. Manuscript grammar

The manuscript consists of atomic blocks separated by a defined delimiter, ordinarily a blank line.

A block is either:

* a **tag block**;
* a **buffer block**.

The compiler never freezes through the middle of a complete block.

### Tag blocks

A tag commits the agency of one fictional actor.

Example:

```text
Anton: sets the cup down. "No."
```

A tag may contain:

* speech;
* voluntary action;
* decisions;
* interiority;
* changes of intention;
* character-specific interpretation;
* durable commitments.

A tag may alter the environment through its own actor.

It should not determine another individually tagged character's voluntary response.

### Buffer blocks

Buffers contain neutral narrative integration:

* setting;
* atmosphere;
* sensory detail;
* elapsed time;
* ongoing motion;
* non-volitional consequences;
* already-established state;
* transitions between commitments.

The guiding rule is:

> Tags apply impulses. Buffers integrate them.

A buffer should not independently introduce a new voluntary commitment for an individually tagged character.

---

## 6. Durable commitments

A tag may authorize an action to continue without requiring repetitive tags.

Example:

```text
Anton: follows Elise until she stops.
```

Later buffers may continue Anton's movement under that already-selected intent.

Conceptually, a durable commitment has:

* actor;
* action;
* target or direction;
* persistence condition;
* termination condition.

These do not need explicit syntax.

They exist to let the manuscript remain fluid without turning every continuing bodily action into another tag.

---

## 7. Aggregate tags

Aggregate tags are permitted only when their membership is ownership-safe.

Examples may include:

```text
Crowd: surges away from the barricade.
```

or:

```text
Guards: lower their spears.
```

An aggregate tag may commit only agents that are currently represented by that aggregate.

It must not silently include an individually tagged character.

Therefore:

* an unindividuated crowd may act as `Crowd:`;
* unnamed guards may act collectively as `Guards:`;
* once a particular guard is promoted into an individually tagged agent, that character's voluntary response belongs to that character's own tag.

Universal tags such as:

```text
Everyone:
```

are discouraged or forbidden because they can silently commit the externally owned character and other individually represented agents.

The general rule is:

> Aggregate syntax may not bypass individual ownership.

---

## 8. External character ownership

Assume `Mara` is externally authored.

The visible manuscript rules do not state that Mara is special.

Mara follows the same fictional tag grammar as every other character.

The hidden ownership layer alone distinguishes her.

### Hard boundary

Generation must be terminated before the model can commit Mara's block.

A lexical stop sequence is the simplest implementation where supported.

The stop should be keyed to reserved tag syntax itself rather than depending on preceding whitespace.

For example:

```text
Mara:
```

may be reserved exclusively for the actual character tag.

This avoids the failure mode where a stop such as:

```text
\n\nMara:
```

misses because Mara is the first token sequence emitted in a new assistant generation and no leading block delimiter is generated.

If ordinary manuscript content could contain the same literal tag sequence, the grammar must prohibit or escape such occurrences.

The required behavior is:

> The model may approach Mara's turn, but it may not generate Mara's committing block.

---

## 9. Human input transformation

The collaborator's real input must not survive as a normal model-visible user turn containing Mara's action.

Instead:

1. generation reaches the Mara boundary;
2. generation stops;
3. the collaborator supplies Mara's block;
4. the host captures that input;
5. the input is transformed into manuscript text;
6. it is merged into the current manuscript immediately after the model-generated material.

Conceptually:

```text
real collaborator input
        ↓
capture
        ↓
Mara manuscript block
        ↓
merged manuscript
```

not:

```text
USER:
Mara says ...
```

The model therefore never receives persistent evidence that:

> Mara = user role.

---

## 10. Human-authored buffers and editing authority

Normal collaborator input after a Mara stop produces Mara's tag block.

It does not implicitly create arbitrary buffer narration.

Separately, the collaborator or editor may modify any material still inside the mutable frontier, including:

* Mara blocks;
* model-authored tags;
* buffers;
* formatting;
* ordering;
* wording.

This editorial authority is distinct from the ordinary external-character capture path.

The rule is therefore:

> **Capture is character-specific; editing is manuscript-wide within the mutable frontier.**

---

## 11. Live generation cycle

A normal cycle is:

```text
canonical history
        ↓
neutral continuation control
        ↓
model generation
        ↓
Mara boundary encountered
        ↓
generation stops
        ↓
collaborator supplies Mara block
        ↓
block merged into manuscript
        ↓
mutable frontier normalized
        ↓
new continuation control
        ↓
generation resumes
```

Mara may appear frequently or rarely depending entirely on the fiction.

A passage may require ten collaborator interventions in a short span.

Another may run for thousands of words without her.

That variation should remain visible only as fictional activity, not as persistent transport structure.

---

## 12. Immediate frontier normalization

The mutable frontier should not accumulate one visible continuation seam per human intervention.

After each collaborator insertion, the next request reconstructs the still-mutable manuscript so earlier live seams disappear.

For example, raw interaction may temporarily have produced:

```text
ASSISTANT:
... Mara: ...

USER:
continue

ASSISTANT:
... Mara: ...

USER:
continue

ASSISTANT:
... Mara: ...
```

The next request should reconstruct the mutable frontier as:

```text
ASSISTANT:
...
Mara: ...
...
Mara: ...
...
Mara: ...

USER:
[neutral continuation]
```

Only the single continuation-control turn currently needed at the edge remains visible.

This distinction is fundamental:

> **Normalization happens every request. Freezing happens only when the frontier reaches its transport target.**

The protocol therefore does not rely on waiting several thousand words before hiding recent collaboration topology.

---

## 13. Continuation control

Continuation-control messages exist only to authorize more manuscript generation.

They should be semantically boring and non-evaluative.

Avoid:

> Excellent. This is good. Continue.

Such wording teaches that each preceding span is a graded or completed artifact.

It may reinforce stylistic drift, mini-conclusions, and approval-seeking prose.

Prefer neutral instructions such as:

> Continue the manuscript directly from the current endpoint. Preserve established causal, stylistic, perspectival, and formatting continuity. Do not recap, restart, summarize, or force resolution.

The desired model interpretation is approximately:

```text
[manuscript] + CONTINUE
```

not:

```text
assistant submitted work
→ user judged it
→ assistant starts another installment
```

Frozen history should preferably use one canonical byte-identical continuation message for stable conditioning and prefix caching.

---

## 14. Generation outcomes

### External-character boundary

The collaborator receives the floor.

### Empty output at the boundary

The model attempted to place Mara immediately next.

The floor remains with the collaborator.

Do not force model text merely to avoid an empty generation.

### Natural completion

Issue another neutral continuation request.

Natural API completion is transport behavior, not scene closure.

### Maximum-output termination

A maximum-output stop may occur mid-block.

The default recovery rule is:

> Roll back the incomplete block to the last complete block boundary and regenerate from there.

If a host can reliably continue an incomplete block without introducing ambiguity, it may do so as an implementation-specific optimization.

The canonical manuscript must not freeze a syntactically incomplete block.

### Safety, recitation, provider interruption, or equivalent exceptional stop

Treat as abnormal transport failure.

Default behavior:

1. discard the incomplete trailing block;
2. restore the last complete block boundary;
3. retry, regenerate, or otherwise recover according to host policy.

Exceptional stop artifacts must not become fictional events.

---

## 15. Human barge-in

The collaborator may insert Mara at any valid block boundary without waiting for the model to request her.

The stop sequence is therefore one trigger for external authorship, not the sole permission mechanism.

Once inserted, the block is normalized into the manuscript exactly as if the model had reached the Mara boundary naturally.

After later reconstruction, stop-triggered and barge-in interventions should be indistinguishable.

---

## 16. Freezing

The mutable frontier is periodically promoted into immutable history.

Freezing is append-only.

Old frozen spans are not normally re-cut because that would:

* disturb historical demonstrations;
* invalidate cache prefixes;
* alter transport statistics retroactively.

### Target size

A host may choose a nominal transport target such as approximately:

```text
3,000–4,200 words
```

with modest jitter.

This number belongs to the compiler, not to the model-visible prompt.

The exact target is advisory.

The compiler may overrun it to reach a better block boundary.

---

## 17. Cut selection

Transport cuts do not need to be statistically independent of all manuscript structure.

That is neither possible nor desirable.

They may intentionally correlate with **low-salience** structural features that provide safe boundaries.

They should avoid correlation with **high-salience** fictional or authorship features.

Preferred locations include:

* mid-passage buffer boundaries;
* ordinary non-climactic transitions;
* boundaries after non-external tags where surrounding material plainly continues.

Avoid:

* immediately before Mara;
* immediately after Mara;
* dense Mara interaction runs;
* scene openings;
* scene closures;
* chapter-like resolutions;
* emotional peaks;
* major POV resets;
* obvious narrative handoffs.

The governing principle is:

> Transport boundaries may correlate with low-salience manuscript structure required for safe cutting, but should be decorrelated from authorship boundaries and high-salience narrative structure.

---

## 18. Three independent horizons

The protocol separates three notions of length.

### Generation horizon

How long a particular model generation runs before stopping naturally or meeting a boundary.

### Authorship horizon

How long the fiction proceeds before Mara next acts.

This is determined by narrative causality.

### Transport horizon

How much normalized mutable manuscript accumulates before a span is frozen.

This is determined by the host/compiler.

These must not be conflated.

Dense Mara activity may create many live interventions without producing short historical chunks.

A long Mara absence may produce long uninterrupted generations without making her structurally unimportant.

---

## 19. Cold start

The first request lacks demonstrations of the desired manuscript behavior.

A manually prepared or hand-corrected seed span is therefore recommended.

It should demonstrate:

* substantial continuous prose;
* tag/buffer alternation;
* multiple characters interacting independently;
* Mara appearing naturally away from transport boundaries;
* dense tag runs;
* durable commitments;
* minor or anonymous agents;
* unresolved scene continuation;
* no artificial handoff toward Mara;
* no conclusion simply because a model generation ends.

The seed should demonstrate literary quality, not merely syntax.

It functions as the initial in-context exemplar.

---

## 20. Linting before freeze

Frozen spans become persistent demonstrations and should therefore be checked before promotion.

Useful lint targets include:

* model-authored Mara commitment;
* buffer-contained voluntary action;
* cross-character commitment inside the wrong tag;
* ownership-unsafe aggregate tags;
* malformed reserved tag syntax;
* incomplete blocks;
* accidental scene closure at a transport cut;
* formatting drift;
* untagged individually represented agents performing committed actions.

Ambiguous bodily or involuntary reactions need not be policed with formal rigidity.

The grammar exists to preserve agency, not to flatten prose into legalese.

---

## 21. Soft railroading

The model may preserve literal Mara ownership while constraining her indirectly through the environment.

For example:

```text
Anton: locks the only exit.

The floor begins collapsing beneath Mara.
```

This protocol does not attempt to make such behavior impossible.

The mutable frontier can be edited, regenerated, or rewritten before freezing.

Soft railroading is therefore treated as a narrative-quality issue rather than a hard ownership violation.

The distinction is:

* **the model may create circumstances affecting Mara;**
* **the model may not commit Mara's voluntary response.**

This preserves causal interaction without turning “protect user agency” into “protect Mara from consequences.”

---

## 22. Reasoning and continuation

Where a model supports substantive reasoning or thinking, continuation may enable it.

Reasoning should be directed primarily toward:

* reconstructing current fictional state;
* tracking what each character knows;
* recovering active motives and durable commitments;
* propagating earlier causes;
* identifying unresolved consequences;
* deciding which characters have reasons to act next.

Reasoning should not be strongly optimized toward:

* predetermined plot destinations;
* forced novelty;
* neat thematic closure;
* designing an ideal ending.

The preferred principle is:

> **Use reasoning to propagate the fictional state forward from causes, not backward from a desired story outcome.**

This keeps character behavior causal rather than teleological.

---

## 23. Host requirements

A compatible host needs equivalent access to the following operations:

* request model continuation;
* enforce a hard external-character boundary;
* capture collaborator input before it becomes persistent raw history;
* reconstruct model-visible history;
* merge external text into manuscript-bearing context;
* insert neutral continuation control;
* maintain a mutable frontier;
* freeze normalized spans into an append-only prefix.

Useful but optional capabilities include:

* streaming;
* provider stop-reason metadata;
* native message editing;
* prefix caching;
* configurable output ceilings;
* stable message identifiers.

If native history editing is unavailable, reconstruction from separately stored canonical manuscript state is sufficient.

The protocol depends on behavior, not on any particular API role names or extension system.

---

## 24. Abstract transformation

The protocol can be summarized as:

```text
live interaction
        ↓
capture collaborator contribution
        ↓
merge into manuscript
        ↓
normalize mutable frontier
        ↓
present one current continuation seam
        ↓
generate more manuscript
        ↓
repeat
        ↓
freeze at low-salience block boundary
        ↓
append canonical chunk to immutable history
```

Real interaction structure is progressively discarded.

The model-visible manuscript retains:

* what happened;
* who did what in the fiction;
* prose;
* causality;
* character behavior.

It discards:

* who typed which block;
* how many requests occurred;
* where stops fired;
* how often the collaborator intervened.

---

## 25. Evaluation

The protocol should be evaluated on both structural concealment and narrative behavior.

### Boundary-identification test

Provide compiled histories to:

* the target model;
* another language model;
* or a classifier.

Ask it to identify:

* where collaborator interventions occurred;
* which historical boundaries corresponded to hard stops;
* which character is externally authored based specifically on transport structure.

Performance attributable to transport features should approach chance.

This does not require that Mara's prose itself be stylistically indistinguishable from model prose.

### Narrative-behavior evaluation

Track whether the model:

* disproportionately ends scenes near Mara;
* repeatedly yields narrative initiative to Mara;
* makes other characters wait for Mara unnecessarily;
* collapses into `Mara → NPC → Mara → NPC` exchange structure;
* avoids NPC↔NPC interaction;
* treats continuation boundaries as mini-endings;
* produces different behavior near frozen chunk edges.

The architecture succeeds when transport structure stops predicting fictional structure.

---

## 26. Core invariants

The complete protocol can be reduced to these invariants:

1. **Commitment occurs through ownership-safe tag blocks.**
2. **The model cannot generate the externally owned character's committing tag.**
3. **Real collaborator input is transformed into ordinary manuscript content rather than preserved as a character-bearing user turn.**
4. **The mutable frontier is reconstructed on every request so old live continuation seams disappear immediately.**
5. **Only one current continuation-control seam need remain at the active edge.**
6. **Frozen spans are append-only and cut only at complete block boundaries.**
7. **Transport cuts avoid Mara and high-salience narrative structure.**
8. **Abnormal termination rolls back to the last complete block unless exact partial continuation is explicitly supported.**
9. **Aggregate tags may not bypass individually tagged character ownership.**
10. **Frozen history preserves fictional causality while discarding real interaction topology.**

---

## 27. Final criterion

Any future feature should be judged by two questions:

> Does this make the model perceive a continuous fictional world, or does it make the model perceive a conversational turn?

and:

> Does this preserve fictional information while discarding unnecessary transport information?

If a mechanism strengthens fictional continuity without revealing collaboration topology, it belongs naturally in the protocol.

If it makes the transport layer narratively legible, it should be reconsidered.
